How Chinese buyers value numeric .com domains — and a desk to buy or sell them.

Why numbers are China's favourite domains

Chinese has no alphabet, and pinyin is spelled inconsistently across dialects. Numbers are universal, fast to type on any phone and easy to say aloud — so many of China's biggest platforms use them: 163.com (NetEase), 360.cn, 58.com, 4399.com.

Scarcity drives value: only 100 two-digit, 1,000 three-digit, 10,000 four-digit and 100,000 five-digit .com names exist. Reported sales include 55.com ($2.3M), 114.com ($2.1M) and 88888.com ($245k).

FAQ

What is a 5N domain?

A five-digit numeric domain such as 67879.com. There are exactly 100,000 possible 5N .com names, and Chinese buyers trade them as liquid digital assets ('chips').

Why do Chinese buyers like numeric domains?

Numbers are understood across dialects and are easier to type and remember than pinyin. Brands like 163.com, 360.cn and 58.com prove the model.

What makes one 5N worth more than another?

No 4, no 0 (often called 'no-4 no-0' or 'chip-grade'), more 8/6/9, patterns like AAAAA, AABBB or ABABA, and meaningful sequences like 168 or 520.
